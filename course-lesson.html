Structuring Content for Semantic Search

Welcome to sub-module 2.2. In this section, we are diving deep into how search engines understand the structure of a webpage. It's no longer just about keyword frequency; it's about context, hierarchy, and semantics.

tips_and_updates

Key Takeaway: Think of heading tags (H1-H6) as an outline for a term paper. They must flow logically. An H3 should always be a sub-topic of the H2 preceding it.

The Role of the H1 Tag

The H1 is your headline. There should generally only be one per page, and it must clearly state the primary topic. Search engine crawlers weigh the words in the H1 heavily when determining the overall context of the document.

  • Keep it under 60 characters for best display in some CMS environments.
  • Ensure it closely matches the user intent of your target keyword.
  • Do not stuff keywords; write for humans first.

Structuring with H2s and H3s

Use H2s to break up major sections of your content. If a section under an H2 becomes too long or complex, break it down further with H3s. This nested structure helps both users skimming the content and bots crawling it.

Example Structure
<h1>Complete Guide to SEO</h1>
<h2>Technical SEO</h2>
<h3>Site Speed Optimization</h3>
<h3>Mobile Responsiveness</h3>
<h2>Content Optimization</h2>

Next, we will look at how to implement Schema markup to explicitly tell search engines what these content blocks mean.
